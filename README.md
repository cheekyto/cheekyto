# Sung Min Yang, Ph.D.

**Neuroscientist** with 17+ years of experience in systems neuroscience, electrophysiology and neuropharmacology. I study how neurons and neural networks work, and how they change in disease and under drug treatment, in human iPSC-derived neurons, 3D brain organoids and rodent models. I build the quantitative workflows that turn neural recordings into decisions.

Currently a Research Associate at **Harvard University** (Department of Stem Cell and Regenerative Biology), Cambridge, MA.

### What I work on
- **Electrophysiology & neural recording:** high-density MEA, patch clamp, slice and in vitro electrophysiology, chronic recordings, spike sorting, burst and network analysis, quality control
- **Neural models:** human iPSC-derived neurons, 3D brain organoids, primary rodent neurons, brain slices, animal models
- **Functional neuroscience:** neuropharmacology and dose-response studies, calcium imaging, optogenetics, chemogenetics, phenotypic screening
- **Data analysis:** MATLAB, Python, R · signal processing, feature extraction, statistics, data visualization

### Featured project
**[CellCircuit](https://github.com/cheekyto/CellCircuit)**: an end-to-end MATLAB pipeline for multielectrode-array recordings of brain organoids, from spike-sorted units to group-level measures of firing, bursting and network bursting, in one command. In its 2026 revision, the slowest quality-control stage went from an estimated ~850 h to ~30 min with identical results. A regression harness tests every stage, and all 232 parameters live in one file that is saved with each recording.
